# Residency and Identity Policy

## Who may apply

Applicants must be a U.S. citizen, a permanent resident (green card holder), or hold a valid temporary visa. The bank does not ask for or consider an applicant's national origin. The only residency question that affects a decision is whether the applicant's legal status will last as long as the loan.

## Declaring residency status

Every application states the applicant's residency status: U.S. citizen, permanent resident, or visa holder. A visa holder also enters the date the visa expires. The bank does not check citizenship against a government website; identity is verified separately (see below) and the declared status is applied to the product rules.

## Visa holders

A visa holder meets the residency requirement when the visa remains valid until after the final payment date of the requested loan term. If the visa ends sooner, the application is not declined automatically: a loan officer refers it to an underwriter, who may approve it with a written condition, for example that the borrower provides proof of renewal before the visa expires. A visa that has already expired cannot be used to apply.

## How identity is verified

The bank confirms who the applicant is using a current government-issued photo ID (driver's license, state ID, or passport), the last four digits of the Social Security number, the date of birth, and the applicant's address. Name, date of birth, and address on the ID must agree with the application. An expired ID is not accepted. The SSN, name, and date of birth are also checked against identity records, and the name is screened against sanctions lists, before any decision is made.

## One open application at a time

Each applicant may have only one open application at a time. A second application from the same person is held until the first is approved, declined, or withdrawn. This does not stop an applicant from applying for another loan after a decision has been made on the earlier one.

## Possible duplicate or reused identity

If the Social Security number (last four digits) and date of birth on an application match an earlier application filed under a different name, the later application is stopped for review as a possible reused identity. Staff cannot approve it and an underwriter cannot waive this check. The earlier application is not affected. The applicant may contact a loan officer to correct a mistyped number.
